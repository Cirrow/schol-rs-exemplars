### Pages 5–14 (Essay Response)

**Selected Question:** Question Two

Religion and spirituality, although extremely different, are both areas which have confused, enlightened, angered and calmed many researchers and people in general over the centuries they have been present in our world. This can be attributed to its self-contradictions, lack of scientific evidence, and taught ignorance, as well as the human influence that repels many away from specifically religion. Contrary to this, the people who learn and research about religion and spirituality can find many rewarding aspects, such as spiritual awakening, religious appreciation, a new understanding of the world as well as new found knowledge about such a largely spread aspect of our history and future (focusing primarily on Christian/Catholic examples).

The Bible is one of the main foundations of religion in our world, written thousands of years ago by many different people through different areas and times. Because of this, as well as new teachings (Encyclicals), there are many contradictions which make it difficult to understand. For example, the greatest commandment, barring "Love thy God", is that of "Love thy neighbour as yourself". This is a simple yet powerful teaching that has stumped many, because the religions which follow the Bible have never truly untaught or lived by that statement. Many people subconsciously add a list of conditions that the Catholic Church and other religious organisations have added on over time. It becomes "Love thy neighbour as long as they have the same beliefs as you and live their lives according to your beliefs and not their own". This mentality makes religion hard to understand and research. Love is a simple word that holds so much meaning; to religion it means treasuring those around you, having kindness for those you disagree with, and having compassion for all. This fact has been tainted by human influence and interpretation within religion. This tainted practice of love confuses and repulses people from religion, including researchers, because it is hard to understand how a practice of people can preach about love then practice the opposite.

Another aspect of religious and spiritual perspectives that makes it a challenging topic to research is the outsider vs. insider mentality and set of experiences. To study religion and spirituality as someone who is not religious nor spiritual is extremely difficult. It can be hard to understand this way of life if you have never lived it. Religion and spirituality are complex, and each individual has a different understanding and connection with their faith. This individualized aspect means that while each religion has an overall code, to each believer that way of life means something totally distinct from the next person. This difference in connection makes it a challenging area to study because the more you learn, the more there is to learn. A researcher who wishes to be thorough would need to spend months just researching different aspects of individual religion.

Studying a religion is extremely difficult for someone who does not partake in that experience. For example, Sigmund Freud, the father of psychoanalysis, created a theory on religion, conveying the message that religion is a "wishful thing that involves the separation from other actions and a continuousness found nowhere else but in hallucination confusion". This quote was written by Freud, an outsider to religion who couldn't fully grasp the idea of individualized religious perspectives and experiences. Researching religion can be extremely rewarding, but to get to the truly rewarding educated aspects of religion, there are layers that need to be peeled away. These layers can look like brick walls to those who don't know what's underneath, creating a hazard and repelling effect for those wanting to research religion.

One of these layers is taught ignorance, which originally was born from not understanding and only seeing the bad and contradicting side to religion or religious figures, combined with the ignorance that science creates. For those who study or have knowledge of science, religion has become something 'disproven' and outdated. In school, students are taught about evolution and biology, and this knowledge can deter people away from understanding or trying to understand religion. To many, science and religion are two ends of a spectrum that can never both be correct. Much of religion is not backed by scientific evidence, meaning to many people it is not factual. This perspective creates an unintentional barrier to researchers delving into the world of religion and spirituality. As Blaise Pascal stated: "If one submits everything to reason, our religion will contain nothing that is mysterious or supernatural." Pascal proves that the elimination of taught ignorance can lead to the knowledge and appreciation of both science and religion. The difficult part is taking the first step to that removal, because many don't step outside of what they understand to do research.

When hesitation and taught ignorance are removed, studying religion and spirituality starts becoming rewarding, giving researchers knowledge of a whole new way of life and a large population of the world.

On the other side, if a researcher is already religious, it can be just as hard because to be a good researcher you need to be unbiased and willing to learn new information. For someone already part of a religion, being unbiased is extremely hard, especially when researching negative aspects or viewpoints against their specific religion. Along with this, when someone has grown up or lived a certain way of life for a substantial amount of time, they start to believe they know everything about it. This belief becomes a barrier to being educated about new subjects. Also, if you are religious in a faith that follows the Bible, teachings like Proverbs 3:5-6 say "Trust in your Lord, not in your own understanding, use his word and his will set straight your path." If a religious researcher followed this word, researching other aspects or religions would be extremely difficult and would most likely result in a biased report.

Contrary to these negative and difficult aspects, there are many rewarding aspects of studying religion. One aspect is religious appreciation. Religion and spirituality are not the same thing, but they can be intertwined; a lot of religion is based on an individual's connection with God through spirituality. Spiritual awakening is becoming aware of your soul and inner self mentally. Spirituality does not necessarily mean believing in a higher power, but believing in yourself and knowing your soul inside and out. Researching religion may not convert the researcher, but it can create a realization of values or morals that spiritually align with you.

Researching religion and spirituality brings religious appreciation, which is important in society because you meet people, family, or partners who are religious. Having an understanding and appreciation for religion helps people understand others in a deeper sense and provides the ability to connect with someone across different experiences.

Furthermore, researching religion and spirituality leads to an understanding and appreciation of our world and society's history. Religion has played an instrumental part in world development. The current society we live in is mostly secular as a common ground. Secular humanism is based on the idea that decisions and understanding should be guided by human experience rather than religion; however, studying religion helps us understand the secular world because secular humanism stemmed from Christianity (specifically the English Church). Understanding the roots of secular humanism and why it was created is extremely beneficial when trying to understand how modern secular countries operate. Understanding our world's history is how we begin to understand our future, and for our secular world, religion is our history.

In a psychological context, religion is preempted and psychologically predictable because the human brain can comprehend our eventual demise, automatically contemplating concepts of life similar to religious attributes. A study by Justin Barrett, PhD (Oxford University), states: "This is showing our cognitive engineered tools biases us towards a certain way of thinking. One of prelife, an afterlife, Gods, invisible beings who are doing things — these are all aspects of most large world religions." This quote shows that researching religion is rewarding not only for historical content, but in understanding ourselves and our human-created fears of demise. It may even provide answers to lifelong fears or ways of thinking.

Finally, Crystal Park notes: "While this work is ongoing, findings to date are unequivocal, various aspects of religion are strongly related to physical and psychological well-being in everyday life in general." This shows how religion is intertwined in the most fundamental aspects of human life. Understanding our history, our 'why', and how we connect in a multicultural, multi-religious society is essential. Without this connection, we are a society of disconnected communities with nothing holding us together. That is what researching religion and spirituality helps create, and why it is so rewarding. Even though it may be hard to research, it remains forever rewarding in more ways than one.
